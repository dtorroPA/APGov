Key terms

Redistricting
Redrawing district lines after each census, every 10 years, so each district has about the same population.
Gerrymandering
Drawing district lines on purpose to help one party or group win more seats.
Packing
Squeezing the other side's voters into a few districts they win by huge margins.
Cracking
Splitting the other side's voters across many districts so they fall just short in each.
Wasted votes & efficiency gap
A vote is "wasted" if it goes to a loser or is extra beyond the 14 a winner needs. The efficiency gap compares each side's wasted votes as a share of all votes.

Supreme Court cases

Baker v. Carr (1962)
Federal courts can hear challenges to unequal districts. Opened the door to "one person, one vote." That's why every district here has exactly 27 voters.
Shaw v. Reno (1993)
Districts drawn mainly by race must pass strict scrutiny under the Equal Protection Clause of the 14th Amendment.
Rucho v. Common Cause (2019)
Partisan gerrymandering claims are political questions that federal courts cannot decide. States, state courts, and Congress can still limit it.
